## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose
This Information Security Policy establishes the framework for protecting Stegano's information assets, ensuring the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of sensitive data while supporting business operations and regulatory compliance requirements.

### 1.2 Scope
This policy applies to:
- All Stegano employees, contractors, and third parties
- All information systems, networks, and data
- All locations where Stegano conducts business
- All cloud services and hosted environments
- All customer data and intellectual property

### 1.3 Objectives
- Protect sensitive information from unauthorized access, use, or disclosure
- Ensure business continuity and minimize security risks
- Comply with applicable laws, regulations, and contractual obligations
- Maintain customer trust and competitive advantage
- Support enterprise growth and market expansion

## 2. Governance and Responsibility

### 2.1 Executive Responsibility
The Chief Executive Officer has ultimate accountability for information security and delegates operational responsibility to the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO).

### 2.2 Information Security Organization
- **Chief Information Security Officer**: Overall information security program leadership
- **Security Team**: Day-to-day security operations and incident response
- **Privacy Officer**: Data privacy and protection oversight
- **Compliance Manager**: Regulatory compliance and audit coordination

### 2.3 Employee Responsibilities
All employees are responsible for:
- Understanding and complying with security policies and procedures
- Protecting information assets and reporting security incidents
- Completing required security awareness training
- Following secure practices in daily work activities

### 2.4 Management Responsibilities
Managers are responsible for:
- Ensuring team compliance with security policies
- Approving access requests and conducting periodic reviews
- Supporting security initiatives and providing necessary resources
- Incident reporting and response coordination

## 3. Information Classification and Handling

### 3.1 Data Classification Levels

#### 3.1.1 Public
- Information intended for public disclosure
- Marketing materials and public documentation
- No restrictions on distribution

#### 3.1.2 Internal
- Information for internal business use
- General business communications and procedures
- Restricted to employees and authorized parties

#### 3.1.3 Confidential
- Sensitive business information
- Customer data and intellectual property
- Financial information and strategic plans
- Requires specific protection measures

#### 3.1.4 Restricted
- Highly sensitive information
- Personal data subject to privacy regulations
- Trade secrets and proprietary algorithms
- Requires highest level of protection

### 3.2 Handling Requirements
- All information must be classified and labeled appropriately
- Access controls must be implemented based on classification
- Encryption required for Confidential and Restricted data
- Secure disposal required for all classified information

## 4. Access Control and Authentication

### 4.1 Access Control Principles
- **Need-to-Know**: Access granted based on job requirements
- **Least Privilege**: Minimum access necessary to perform duties
- **Separation of Duties**: Critical functions require multiple people
- **Regular Review**: Periodic validation of access rights

### 4.2 Authentication Requirements
- **Multi-Factor Authentication**: Required for all privileged accounts
- **Strong Passwords**: Minimum 12 characters with complexity requirements
- **Single Sign-On**: Centralized authentication where possible
- **Account Lockout**: Automatic lockout after failed attempts

### 4.3 Authorization Management
- **Role-Based Access**: Access based on job functions
- **Approval Process**: Manager approval required for access requests
- **Provisioning**: Standardized user provisioning procedures
- **Deprovisioning**: Immediate access removal upon termination

## 5. Asset Management and Protection

### 5.1 Asset Inventory
- Comprehensive inventory of all information assets

### 5.2 Asset Protection
- **Physical Security**: Secure facilities and equipment protection

### 5.3 Acceptable Use

- Limited personal use with approval

- Monitoring and enforcement procedures

## 6. Cryptography and Key Management

### 6.1 Cryptographic Standards
- **Encryption Algorithms**: FIPS 140-2 approved algorithms only
- **Key Lengths**: Minimum AES-256 for symmetric, RSA-4096 for asymmetric
- **Protocols**: TLS 1.3 for transmission, current NIST standards
- **Post-Quantum**: Preparation for quantum-resistant algorithms

### 6.2 Key Management
- **Generation**: Cryptographically secure random generation
- **Storage**: Hardware security modules (HSM) where possible
- **Distribution**: Secure key exchange protocols
- **Rotation**: Regular key rotation based on usage and risk
- **Destruction**: Secure key destruction when no longer needed

### 6.3 Digital Signatures
- PKI-based digital signatures for authentication
- Code signing for software integrity
- Document signing for legal and contractual purposes
